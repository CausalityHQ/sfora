# COLD INDEPENDENT ADJUDICATION — CNW (Conditional Nuisance Whitening), Pass 40

## VERDICT: **DEAD**

**Earliest failed gate: Gate 2 — the exact zero set.**

**Decisive mechanism-level reason:** `L_CNW` is a functional of the *pooled* second moment `A = Σ_c Σ_{i∈c} r̂_i r̂_iᵀ`, but every argument in §2.2 that motivates it is a statement about `uᵀΣ_W u` for a *single unseen identity*. These are different objects, and the gap is not a technicality — it is occupied by an explicitly constructible, PA-compatible global minimum. Partition `R^512` into `C_b` mutually orthogonal 3-dimensional subspaces `V_c` (feasible: `3·C_b = 90 ≤ 512`, and globally feasible for CUB at `3·100 = 300` and Cars196 at `3·98 = 294`). Place each class's `m=4` residuals as a regular tetrahedron in its own `V_c` and each class mean `μ_c` in the remaining 422-dim complement. Then `A_c = (4‖r̂‖²/3)P_{V_c}`, so `A` is exactly `(4‖r̂‖²/3)` times a rank-90 projector: **`D̂ = D_max`, `L_CNW = 0` exactly**, in every batch. In that same configuration `s(z_i, p̂_c) = ‖μ_c‖` identically for all `i ∈ c`, so `L_PA` is simultaneously at its compactness optimum. And each identity's conditional covariance is `Σ_W^(c) = (ρ²/3)P_{V_c}` — **rank 3 out of 512, participation ratio 3, maximally anisotropic.**

So the loss attains its exact global minimum, in agreement with its own base loss, at the precise opposite of the property the proposal claims it induces. Pooled spectral flatness does not imply per-identity covariance isotropy — an average of anisotropic rank-3 PSD matrices is isotropic under a mild allocation condition — and it supplies no supervision whatsoever that transfers to an unseen identity, for which no allocation was learned. This is not the `D5` case: `D5` analyses a *shared* subspace `V` and argues benignity from "zero conditional noise outside `V`." That argument does not extend, because in the class-private case the union of noise subspaces spans 300 dims on CUB and a novel identity has no reason to land outside it. §1.3's "second structural property" — that `L_PA` drives residuals into `S⊥` so the constraint auto-aims at the ignored subspace — is satisfied *exactly* by this construction, so the proposal's own claimed dynamics lead into it rather than away from it.

The zero set is therefore not `{Σ_W ∝ I}`. It is `{pooled direction scatter ∝ P_{D_max}}`, which contains the target as a measure-zero special case and contains per-identity rank-3 anisotropy as an open, PA-preferred region. No control in C1–C10 distinguishes them, and no instrumentation measures per-identity spectrum in a way that could (see Gate 8).

## Gate-by-gate record

**Gate 1 — Executability: PASSES, with three recorded defects.**

The loss, rank ceiling, computation, sampler, gate, and warm-up are uniquely executable. I independently reproduce the core algebra:
- `rank(A) ≤ B − C_b` is **correct** — per-class residuals sum to zero, positive rescaling preserves span, so `span{r̂_i} = span{r_i}` has dim `≤ Σ_c(m_c−1)`. Hence `D̂ ∈ [1, D_max]` and `L_CNW ∈ [0, log D_max]` as claimed. ✓
- `∂L_CNW/∂r̂_i = 4A r̂_i/‖A‖_F² − 4r̂_i/tr(A)` is **correct** (from `∂L/∂A = 2A/‖A‖_F² − 2I/tr A`, symmetric, doubled by the outer product). ✓
- The stated stationary condition `A r̂_i = (‖A‖_F²/tr A) r̂_i` is correct, **but incomplete**: after the tangential Jacobian, the gradient w.r.t. `r_i` vanishes whenever `r̂_i` is *any* eigenvector of `A`, at *any* eigenvalue. The stationary set is far larger than the flat configurations, and includes the Gate-2 construction.

Defects: (a) **the stated Jacobian is wrong for `ε ≠ 0`.** With `s = √(‖r‖²+ε²)` and the proposal's own non-unit `r̂ = r/s`, the correct form is `∂r̂/∂r = (I − r̂r̂ᵀ)/s`; the document writes `(I − r̂r̂ᵀ·‖r‖²/s²)/s`, double-applying the correction (~4% error on the rank-1 part at the gate threshold, 2×10⁻⁴ at `‖r‖≈0.7`). (b) Consequently `∂L/∂r_i ⊥ r_i` is **not exact** — the leakage is `rᵀ∂L/∂r_i = (ε²/s³)rᵀg ≠ 0`. "Exactly" is false; "to 2×10⁻⁴" is true. (c) **Empty/small-`K` is undefined**: `|K| = 0` gives `D̂ = 0/0` and `L_CNW = +∞`. `F9` voids a *run* at `|K|/B < 0.9` but never defines the loss on the degenerate batch. All three are repairable and none is decisive.

**Gate 2 — Zero set: FAILS.** See above. Additionally, the cheapest configuration is not merely admissible, it is *cheaper for the base loss* than true isotropy: confining residuals to a low-dimensional private subspace orthogonal to the class mean is exactly what compactness plus separation rewards.

**Gate 3 — Direction second moment vs. residual covariance: FAILS independently.**

`A = Σ r̂r̂ᵀ` is the second moment of near-unit *directions*; `Σ_W = E[rrᵀ]` is the covariance. Flatness of one implies nothing about the other. If residual magnitude is systematically larger along one axis and small across many others, `A` is flat while `Σ_W` is rank-1-dominated. D4's "equal weighting" is precisely the property that severs the two objects — the proposal treats scale-freeness as a virtue without noticing it discards exactly the information `Σ_W` is made of. Every argument in §2.2 is about `Σ_W`; the loss touches only `A`; the bridge is never built.

This also makes the instrumentation self-inconsistent: `F2` falsifies on `D̂(Σ_W^unseen)` — the *covariance* participation ratio — while the loss optimizes the *direction* participation ratio. The objective is not guaranteed to move its own pre-registered falsifier in either direction.

The "trace preservation" claim is separately unsound. On the sphere, `(1/m)Σ‖r_i‖² = 1 − ‖μ_c‖²` identically, so `tr(Σ_W)` is a strict function of the class-mean norm — the very quantity `L_PA` maximizes. Orthogonality of `∂L/∂r` to `r` is an instantaneous first-order statement in *residual* space; the update lives in `θ` space through `∂z/∂e = (I−zzᵀ)/‖e‖`, and no first-order orthogonality in `r` constrains the induced change in `tr(Σ_W)`. Even in `r` space, a step orthogonal to `r` *increases* `‖r‖` at second order. `O(d)`-invariance (D3) is correct and is a genuine property; "CNW cannot inflate or shrink `tr(Σ_W)`" is not.

**Gate 4 — Bayes / Jensen / minimax / SNR: FAILS.**

I derived the convexity range independently. With `ε(v) = Φ(−a v^{−1/2})`:
`ε'(v) = (a/2)v^{−3/2}φ(a/√v) > 0`, and `ε''(v) = ε'(v)·(a² − 3v)/(2v²)`.
**`ε` is strictly convex iff `a/√v > √3 ≈ 1.732`, not `≳ 1.5`.** This is not a rounding issue: at the number the proposal quotes, `ε` is strictly *concave*, and Jensen **reverses** — anisotropy at fixed trace would be *beneficial*. The proposal states its own boundary on the wrong side of its own sign change.

Worse, Jensen requires convexity across the whole support of `v`. With the anisotropy the proposal claims to be fixing (`PR ≈ 10` of 512), `v` spans two orders of magnitude and the support straddles `√3`. And the **amplifier premise contradicts argument (i) rather than supplementing it**: if `u` concentrates in high-variance directions (`o > 1`), then (a) the load-bearing equality `E_u[uᵀΣ_W u] = c/d` for every fixed-trace `Σ_W` fails, since it holds only under uniform `u`; and (b) the operating point moves to `v ≈ c/10`, i.e. `a/√v` falls by `√51 ≈ 7×` into the deeply concave region. §2.2 asserts the amplifier is "not required" and that failing it merely shrinks the effect toward "the Jensen floor." The converse — that the amplifier *holding* destroys argument (i) — is unstated. **There is no regime in which a valid proof and a large effect coexist.**

The minimax result (ii) is mathematically correct (`min_Σ max_{‖u‖=1} uᵀΣu` s.t. `tr Σ = c` is uniquely `(c/d)I`), but the fixed-trace budget is not an available constraint: `tr(Σ_W) = 1 − ‖μ_c‖²` is a free lever the base loss already pulls, and reducing it lowers `v` for *every* `u` — a first-order, direction-independent gain, against which flattening at fixed trace is second-order. The theory therefore gives no reason at all to expect CNW to beat control **C4** (trace-only), and §1.3's exact-orthogonality claim, if true, would mean CNW deliberately declines the stronger lever. The Bayes-optimality premise additionally requires *common* class-conditional covariance — the homogeneity assumption the Gate-2 zero set violates and CNW never enforces.

## Correct subcomponents, preserved separately from the verdict

These survive and should not be discarded with the proposal:

1. **The rank ceiling `rank(A) ≤ B − C_b`** and the `D_max` normalization that makes `λ` transfer across `B, m, d`. Clean and correct.
2. **The `O(d)`-invariance argument (D3).** `D̂(RAR^T) = D̂(A)` is exact, and the contrast with VICReg / Barlow Twins / decorrelated-BN — which penalize off-diagonal mass in a canonical basis and are therefore satisfiable by rotation — is a genuine and useful distinction. Any successor targeting spectral shape should keep this property.
3. **The Gram trick.** `‖A‖_F² = ‖G‖_F²`, `tr(A) = Σ_i G_ii`, never materializing `A`. Correct and genuinely near-free.
4. **The D1 profitability bound.** The structure of the argument — deriving an admissible `λ` range from the ratio of `L_PA`'s response to the maximum obtainable `λ·log D_max` — is the right shape for a degeneracy argument, and `λ < α/log D_max` is a real bound rather than a tuned constant.
5. **Convexity of `Φ(−a/√v)` does hold in the realistic retrieval regime** — but for a reason the proposal did not give. With ~5.9k CUB distractors, `R@1 ≈ 0.73` implies pairwise `ε ≈ 5×10⁻⁵` and `a/√v ≈ 3.9 ≫ √3`. The many-distractor argument rescues convexity at the *typical* `u`; it does not rescue it across the support, and it does not survive the amplifier premise.
6. **The predicted signature** — unseen `I(f)` up, `tr(Σ_W)` flat, train R@1 flat-to-down while test R@1 rises — is a well-posed discriminator between a generalization mechanism and better fitting, and is worth retaining independently of CNW.
7. **C6/C7 as a decisive pair**, and the honesty of §5's SOP non-crossing, §6's contamination disclosure, and the refusal to claim In-Shop. These are the correct instincts.

- The Gate-2 construction proves the zero set contains per-identity-anisotropic global minima and that they are PA-compatible. It does **not** prove SGD converges there from ImageNet initialization. That is an empirical question. But the burden runs the other way: the proposal's causal argument requires the zero set to *identify* per-identity isotropy, and it does not.

## Disposition

**DEAD at Gate 2**, with independent failures at Gates 3, 4, 7, 8 and 9 that do not depend on Gate 2. The failure is structural, not editorial: the loss constrains a pooled, seen-identity statistic — the same statistic WCCN fits — while the theory that motivates it is about the conditional covariance of one unseen identity, and the two are joined only by a homogeneity assumption the objective neither enforces nor measures.

Repairing this means changing the supervised object (a per-identity penalty, or a genuine function-level isotropy constraint with an estimator valid at `n ≪ d`), replacing the Jensen argument with one whose convexity range is derived and whose prior is consistent with the claimed effect size, and re-deriving the novelty case against WCCN/PLDA on the new target. That is a **new proposal**. Per the frozen-object rule, it must not be used to upgrade this one.
