## Verdict: **DEAD**

**Earliest failed gate: Gate 1 — measurement provenance of the causal claim.**

**Decisive mechanism-level reason.** The supplied statistics (0.7675 top‑1 error overlap, 0.8085 gallery‑row agreement, 0.6476 same‑wrong‑identity) establish only that errors are *seed‑persistent*. PSPL never measures a cause; §2.1 derives one algebraically from the loss functional. And its two pillars do not do the work claimed: pillar 1 (rotation/relabel invariance) is specific to free prototypes, but pillar 2 — "unseen inputs are unconstrained" — is a property of **every** empirical risk that is a function of training‑support outputs, **including PSPL's own**. Pillar 2 is the one that yields "unseen‑class R@1 collapses to chance"; PSPL removes only pillar 1.

The checkable consequence: because separation repair enforces $h_{\min}=24>0$, the code $c\mapsto\mathbf k^{(c)}$ is **injective on training labels**. So the map "identify the training class privately → linear lookup of $\mu(\mathbf k^{(c)})$" attains $f=\mu(\mathbf k^{(c)})$ exactly, i.e. **the pure‑memorization solution sits at PSPL's exact global minimum**, identically to CosFace. PSPL therefore cannot "change the ranking… by unseen‑class separability" (§2.1). It reparameterizes the target. The proposal concedes the premise (§7 closing: "the code is a deterministic function of the label, so the Shannon content of supervision is unchanged") and then asserts the conclusion anyway.

## Audit findings

**1. Gate‑1 provenance — FAILED.** Inferred from protocol algebra alone. No measurement of prototype coordinates, prototype‑table rank, private heads, or label‑code digit transfer. The three supplied statistics are equally consistent with a shared ImageNet prior, benchmark resolution, or augmentation‑invariance cause — none excluded. Worse, §2.1's mechanism is *self‑undermining*: PSPL's loss depends on $\theta$ only through training‑support outputs, so the §2.1 pillar‑2 construction applies to PSPL verbatim.

**2. FCS/FPC recurrence — CONFIRMED, third occurrence.** Reducing independently: Pass 39 FCS = normalized blocks + balanced Hamming‑separated multi‑symbol class code + per‑block symbol prediction + code update from affinities. Pass 46 FPC = shared multi‑codebook product class proxies + balanced learned assignment. PSPL is their union with block targets frozen to simplex vertices. Freezing removes parameters, not the reduction: the code stays an injective recoding of training labels. Fixed orthogonal simplex blocks, equal energy, min‑cost assignment, and hysteresis change the *hypothesis class of the target*, not the *supervision object*. Not a new supervision object.

Quantitatively the restriction is near‑vacuous: $9^{64}\approx1.2\times10^{61}$ lattice points for $C\le11{,}318$ classes, with the assignment itself learned. Random balanced codes give pairwise cosine $\approx 1-56.9(9/512)\approx 0$, s.d. $\approx0.044$ — i.e. **PSPL's training targets are statistically a random near‑orthogonal frame**, which is what a CosFace prototype matrix converges to anyway. C3 is therefore "fixed random prototypes" (Hoffer et al. 2018; Pernici et al. RPN), and PSPL is C3 plus a re‑assignment.

**3. Prior art — narrowest defensible novelty is a four‑way conjunction, and two claimed distinctions are wrong.**
- Fixed simplex classifier is **Pernici et al., Regular Polytope Networks** (CVPRW 2019 / TNNLS 2021), which fixes the classifier to $d$‑simplex/$d$‑cube/$d$‑orthoplex vertices and motivates it by *stationary, maximally separated* embeddings — predating Yang et al. (NeurIPS 2022), the proposal's only fixed‑classifier citation. §1.3's "$G{=}1,K{=}C$ reduces exactly to Yang et al." should read "to a regular‑simplex fixed classifier," and only where $C-1\le d$.
- **Wang et al., "Deep Hashing with Minimal‑Distance‑Separated Hash Centers" (CVPR 2023)** already imposes a **hard minimum pairwise distance between class codes for retrieval**, with a Gilbert–Varshamov feasibility analysis PSPL lacks. §3's distinction from Evron et al. — "a hard $h_{\min}$ and balance constraint they do not have" — is refuted at the $h_{\min}$ half.
- **Codebook‑Centric Deep Hashing / CRH (AAAI; arXiv 2511.12162)** "dynamically reassigns hash centers from a **preset codebook** while jointly optimizing the hash function" — i.e. frozen codebook + online feature‑coupled class→codeword reassignment for retrieval. This is PSPL's E/M loop.
- **Deep N‑ary ECOC** (arXiv 2009.10465) supplies $K$‑ary meta‑class groups over a **shared** backbone. **CSQ** (CVPR 2020) supplies frozen per‑class Hadamard centers as retrieval targets. **SwAV / SeLa** supply balanced Sinkhorn assignment.

Narrowest defensible novelty: *the product of $G$ mutually orthogonal $K$‑vertex simplex ETFs as the class‑target set, with an equal‑energy entropy term and a column‑balanced online assignment, applied to unseen‑class DML rather than closed‑set retrieval.* Each conjunct is prior; the novelty is the conjunction plus the evaluation regime. **Product‑simplex geometry plus online assignment is not substantive after FCS/FPC** — FPC already had product codebooks with balanced learned assignment; the delta is freezing the codewords, which is RPN applied per block.

**4. Core causal implication — FAILS, and D3's headline sentence is refuted by the method's own step 4.**
Sharing a digit among $\approx C/K$ labels constrains the **decoding** direction (code → class), not the **encoding** direction (image → code). The network need not decode. `ceil(log_K C)` bounds *code length*, not computation: under memorization the "shared functional" for codeword $(g,k)$ is the disjunction $\mathbb 1[c\in\text{cell}(g,k)]$, computed by private class identification followed by an OR — attaining the exact global minimum.

Read literally, "**no single group can separate any pair of classes on its own**" is **false, and is the exact negation of $h_{\min}$**: separation repair guarantees every class pair differs in $\ge 24$ of the 64 groups, so every pair is separated by at least 24 groups acting individually. Balance yields only the weaker "no single group *identifies* a class."

Additional inversion: the E‑step's *targets move every 5 epochs* (churn until ~epoch 120). A genuine shared visual functional has its targets pulled out from under it; a lookup table re‑indexes for free. The stated optimization bias runs **toward** memorization.

**5. Lemma 1 CORRECT; corollary DOES NOT FOLLOW.** I re‑derived Lemma 1 independently — $\|\mu\|=1$, $\langle\mu(\mathbf k),\mu(\mathbf k')\rangle=1-\frac hG\frac{K}{K-1}$, slope $9/512=0.0175781$, range $[-1/8,1]$ — all exact.

The corollary needs four unproved steps, and Lemma 1 gives none of them: (i) *lands near a lattice point* — under the equal‑energy constraint the nearest‑lattice cosine of a generic direction is $\overline{\max_k\langle\hat u_g,s_k\rangle}\approx0.5$ (≈58°, concentrated to ±0.03 over 64 groups); the lattice is a coarse quantizer, not a cover; (ii) *near an **unoccupied** point* — nothing assigns unseen identities anywhere, and unoccupied slots receive no gradient, ever; (iii) *its images agree* — no term constrains unseen inputs (§2.1 pillar 2 again); (iv) *receives a margin* — $m$ applies only to an assigned training target. And decisively: **all code objects are discarded at deployment**, where the metric is $\langle f(x_q),f(x_g)\rangle$ between test descriptors; Lemma 1 is a property of a discarded object. Finally, "free‑prototype losses have no analogue" is vacuously false: $S^{511}$ is homogeneous, so every direction already has identical margin structure to every other — the sphere is *more* index‑free than the lattice.

**6. Proposition 2 — invalid proof, conclusion true only to $O(\epsilon)$; §1.4 Jacobian is wrong.**
- $q(u)=u/(\|u\|+\epsilon)$ has $\|q\|=\|u\|/(\|u\|+\epsilon)<1$ **always**. So $\hat u_g$ is never on the unit sphere and never equals a simplex vertex. §1.3's "Because $\hat u_g$ is unit norm, $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grp}}$ is invariant to $\|u_g\|$" is false; $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grp}}$ is strictly decreasing in each $\|u_g\|$. The infimum is therefore **not attained at** $f=\mu(\mathbf k)$; at $\mu(\mathbf k)$, $\|u_g\|=\rho=1/8$ and $\langle\hat u_g,s_{k_g}\rangle=1-8\times10^{-6}$.
- Exact Jacobian: $J_q=\frac1{n+\epsilon}\!\left(I-\frac{n}{n+\epsilon}vv^\top\right)$, $n=\|u\|$, $v=u/n$. §1.4's $\frac{1}{\|u_g\|}(I-\hat u_g\hat u_g^\top)$ is the Jacobian of *exact* normalization, not of the implemented $q$; they agree only to $O(\epsilon/n)$. **At $u=0$:** $q$ *is* differentiable with $J_q(0)=I/\epsilon=10^{6}I$, whereas §1.4's expression divides by zero. So D1's "the $1/\|u_g\|$ factor amplifies gradients into starved groups" saturates at $10^{6}$ — and with $\gamma/G=0.1875$ and global clipping at 5.0, a single deeply starved group would consume the entire clipped budget and zero out the other 63. The stated D1 attack and the stated clipping interact destructively in exactly D1's regime.
- Proof defects independent of $\epsilon$: the denominator drops the margin ($\log\sum_ke^{\gamma\langle\hat u,s_k\rangle}$ instead of the margined form); and "log‑sum‑exp convexity along geodesics" is not a valid step — Euclidean convexity on $\mathbb R^{K-1}$ does not give geodesic convexity on $S^{K-2}$ (a linear function on a sphere is not geodesically convex). The gradient computation itself is right (I verified the radial cancellation via $\sum_{k\ne k^*}s_k=-s_{k^*}$).
- **Equal‑energy gradient:** $\partial\mathcal L_{\mathrm{eng}}/\partial f=2\sum_g(\log\pi_g+1)U_gu_g$ is correct, but §1.4 folds $\lambda_e$ into a quantity labeled $\partial\mathcal L_{\mathrm{eng}}/\partial f$ while $\partial\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grp}}/\partial f$ carries no coefficient. After projection: $\sum_gU_gu_g=UU^\top f=f$, so at uniform $\pi$ the gradient is purely radial and $(I-ff^\top)$ annihilates it exactly — self‑consistent. Unremarked cost: $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{eng}}=0$ forces every descriptor onto a 448‑dimensional submanifold of $S^{511}$ (63 dimensions removed), a per‑image constraint on the deployed representation that is never audited.

**7. E‑step — multiple hard failures; the "hard constraint" is not enforced.**
- **$\log$ of zero counts: fatal and guaranteed on CUB.** Step 3 adds $\log n_{gk,g'k'_c}$ with **no smoothing anywhere**. On CUB, $C=100$, $K=9$: a group pair's joint table has 81 cells with marginals $\approx11$ and total 100 — most cells are zero. $\log 0=-\infty$ drives the assignment cost to $-\infty$ and the min‑cost flow collapses onto empty cells. Even without exact zeros, 63 PMI terms at $\lambda_{\mathrm{ind}}=0.1$ swamp a cosine cost of range 2.
- **Hysteresis voids balance and $h_{\min}$.** Steps run 2 (balance) → 4 (repair) → 5 (per‑class revert), with **no re‑check after reverting**. Reverting an arbitrary subset of classes to old digits breaks both column sums and pairwise Hamming. Step 4's own "retain the previous code" fallback does the same. So D3's "central structural guarantee… enforced by a hard constraint, not a penalty" is not enforced on the final code table.
- **Sinkhorn fallback breaks the same guarantee.** The proposal offers "entropic Sinkhorn followed by rounding for large $C$" — rounding does not preserve the balance box, precisely on SOP/In‑Shop where sharing is claimed largest.
- **$h_{\min}$ is essentially inactive.** Random balanced codes have mean pairwise Hamming $64\cdot\frac89=56.9$, s.d. $2.51$; $h=24$ is 13.1 s.d. below the mean. Repair almost never fires, which further weakens the §3 novelty claim against Evron et al. No termination or monotonicity argument is given for the $\le20$ passes; "next‑best **feasible** codeword," "the group with the smallest **margin loss**," and tie‑breaking are all undefined.
- **Sharing minima do not follow from the frozen $\beta$ rule.** $\beta=\max(1,\lceil0.25C/K\rceil)$ gives minimum column sums: CUB **8** ✓ (matches); Cars ($C{=}98$) **7**, not $\ge 8$; SOP ($C{=}11{,}318$) **942**, not $\ge1000$; In‑Shop ($C{=}3997$) **332**, not $\ge1000$ — off by 3×. Only CUB's stated number is right.
- **Self‑confirmation (D6) unattacked.** The cost is "assign each class to the codeword its features already prefer" and hysteresis accepts only self‑improving moves. The stated defense — "constrained, so it cannot select a trivially‑satisfied code" — is circular: a memorizing network realizes *any* balanced code exactly, so every balanced code is trivially satisfied. The constraint bites only if the network is not memorizing, which is what was to be shown.
- **Cost/memory of $C\times576$ by $576\times C$:** at SOP this is $1.28\times10^8$ entries (≈0.5 GB fp32), 147 GFLOP per pass, up to 20 passes.

**8. Controls — two of the four load‑bearing ones are infeasible or unmatched.**
- **C2 is infeasible on SOP and In‑Shop.** $G{=}1,K{=}C$ needs a $C$‑vertex simplex in $\mathbb R^{C-1}$: SOP 11,317 dims, In‑Shop 3,996 dims, versus $d=512$. Yet §5 **forecasts C2 SOP $=0.803\pm0.003$** — a frozen number for a control that cannot exist.
- **C9 is not executable as frozen.** Max Hamming distance is $G$, so $h_{\min}=24$ is impossible at $G=8,16$ and $h_{\min}=40$ is impossible at $G=8,16,32$. Fixing $G(K-1)=512$ gives $K=512/G+1$: $65, 33, 17, 9, 5$ — so **$K=9$ is not preserved**, and the sweep confounds $G$ with $K$, with cell coarseness, and with $\gamma=12,m=0.15$ frozen for $K=9$ (a transferability problem §6 itself flags). At $G{=}8$ on CUB, $C/K=1.54$ and $\beta=1$ give column sums in $[0,3]$ — the group becomes a near‑class‑identifier, i.e. the sweep endpoint *is* the D3 degeneracy. F6's "$G=1$" reference point is not in the sweep and would need $K=513>C$.
- **C4 is infeasible or unmatched.** Private digits from the frozen lattice require $K\ge C$ per group ($G(C-1)\gg512$) — infeasible. Under the only workable reading (learned private group codewords) C4 has $C\times512$ free parameters — exactly the prototype matrix §6 boasts of eliminating ($+0.051$M CUB, $+5.79$M SOP) — so it is compute‑matched but **not** parameter‑ or capacity‑matched, and it collapses to a per‑group‑normalized C1. A PSPL > C4 result then confounds sharing with fewer free parameters.
- **C3/C4 cannot distinguish shared visual computation from deterministic label coding.** Both leave memorization at the global optimum; both are injective label recodings. PSPL > C3 is equally explained by the E‑step handing over *easier*, already‑aligned targets (self‑confirmation as a curriculum). No control measures computation.
- **Held‑out cell targets are circular.** A held‑out identity has no cell; obtaining one requires computing $\bar f_c$ from its labeled images and taking $\arg\max_k\langle\widehat{U_g^\top\bar f_c},s_k\rangle$ — fitting the labeled centroid. F7 then measures within‑class consistency of a group arg‑max, which a memorizing network with ImageNet features passes; the $2\times$ chance bar (0.222) is far too weak to discriminate.
- **C13** is called a "reproduction" while §7.2 concedes PFML's optimizer, schedule, head, and proxy rule are unknown — it is a re‑implementation from a description the author states they do not have.

**9. Cost claims — one defensible, two not; assignment failure is non‑deterministic.**
- FLOPs: $U^\top f$ is $2\cdot512^2=0.52$ MFLOP (0.26 MMAC). The quoted "0.3 MFLOP" is the **MAC** count set against a FLOP denominator; under a consistent convention the ratio is ~0.006% (MACs) or ~0.013% (FLOPs). <0.01% survives only under the MACs reading. Minor.
- Wall time <0.1%: **not credible.** The estimate omits that ICM ordering makes the 64 min‑cost flows **sequential** ($CK=101{,}862$ arcs each at SOP — not "milliseconds"), omits the PMI penalty's $C\!\cdot\!K\!\cdot\!G^2/2\approx2.1\times10^8$ lookups, and prices repair as one matmul when up to 20 passes each re‑materialize $C\times C$ (≈2.9 TFLOP and ~10 GB of traffic at SOP).
- ~1 MB: **understated by ~500×.** $U$ = 1.05 MB ✓, but the accounting omits the $C\times C$ Hamming matrix (~0.5 GB fp32 at SOP) and the $C\times512$ EMA centroid table (23 MB at SOP). That centroid table is exactly the size of the prototype matrix PSPL claims to have removed, and it *is* a per‑class private 512‑D coordinate that determines the supervision target — a stop‑gradient/momentum proxy. The "zero learned prototypes / negative parameter count" claim is a statement about the autograd graph, not about the algorithm's state. **§2.1 names class‑private coordinates as the disease; §1.5 keeps one per class and derives the target from it.**
- Assignment failure behavior: **not deterministic.** $\log 0\to-\infty$ (unguarded), Sinkhorn rounding with no balance repair, undefined feasibility/tie‑breaking in step 4, and unchecked constraint violation after step 5.

**10. Frontier arithmetic and protocol — FAILED on every mandated element.**
- **In‑Shop‑first screen not run.** In‑Shop is labeled *secondary* and Cars196 *decisive*, inverting the mandate. The corrected In‑Shop protocol and the verified PA anchor are never engaged; §6 mentions only "a fixed query/gallery split."
- **No current‑digest same‑seed PA control.** C1 is CosFace, C13 is PFML; the In‑Shop claim is parity against PA+DADA's published 0.930 with uncertainty the proposal itself says is unreported.
- **No raw vs. independently selected/final plan.** §5 reports one point per dataset; §2.3 defines a selection protocol but no dual reporting.
- **No out‑of‑sample stage.** §2.3's held‑out identities carry *all* model selection, all falsification thresholds, and all diagnostics — they are in‑sample for the method and cannot also confirm it.
- **Replication is self‑refuted.** By its own forecasts: crossing on Cars only, explicit non‑crossing on SOP, parity on In‑Shop, underpowered CUB. No second dataset replicates.
- **Arithmetic:** the $z$/$p$ values check out exactly (Cars 5‑seed combined SEM 0.00256, $z=2.74$, $p=0.006$; 10‑seed 0.00207, $z=3.38$, $p=0.0007$; CUB 1.53/0.13 and 1.93/0.054). But "**≈35 seeds would be needed for $p<0.05$ at $\Delta=0.004$**" is **wrong**: with PFML's SEM fixed at $0.003/\sqrt5=0.001342$, $p<0.05$ needs combined SEM $\le0.002041$, i.e. $n\ge10.6$ — **11 seeds**, inside the stated 10‑seed headline budget and consistent with their own $z=1.93$ at $n=10$. The claim "CUB cannot deliver a decisive crossing at any realistic seed budget" is false on the proposal's own inputs, and it is the premise for making Cars decisive.
- **Lane matching fails.** C1 at 0.702/0.886 sits ~3 points below PFML's 0.734/0.927, so the forecast +3.6/+4.8 over C1 is mostly a recipe gap; the mechanism margin over the reference is +0.4/+0.7. §7.2 concedes the reference recipe is unknown, then §5 runs the crossing arithmetic anyway.
- **F3/F4 are underpowered equivalence tests with no equivalence procedure.** A $|\Delta|<0.3$ pt bound against a paired 5‑seed difference SEM of ~0.22 pt gives a 90% CI half‑width of ~0.41 pt > 0.3. The two *decisive* falsifiers cannot fire correctly.
- **No forecast is derived from measured premises.** The points, the 80% CIs, and the subjective probabilities are judgmental and mutually inconsistent: Cars CI $[0.920,0.943]$ around 0.934 implies $P(\text{mean}>0.927)\approx0.74$–$0.84$, but the frozen probability is 0.62.

## Correct subcomponents (preserved separately from the verdict)

1. **Lemma 1** — independently re‑derived, exact, including the $0.0175781$ slope, the $[-1/8,1]$ range, and $\|\mu\|=1$.
2. **Proposition 2's conclusion** holds to $O(\epsilon/\rho)\approx10^{-5}$, despite an invalid proof — a false proof, not a false conclusion.
3. **The equal‑energy gradient** and its exact radial cancellation under $(I-ff^\top)$ at uniform $\pi$.
4. **$\lceil\log_K C\rceil$** = 3/3/5 for CUB/Cars/SOP; **CUB minimum sharing = 8** under the frozen $\beta$ rule; $K^G\approx1.2\times10^{61}$; $\gamma(1+1/8)=13.5$; class‑mean subspace 99 on CUB.
5. **D1's irreducible‑cost argument** is correct and in fact conservative — with the margin, a starved group costs $\gamma m+\log(e^{-\gamma m}+K-1)=3.90$ nats, not $\log 9=2.20$.
6. **Parameter arithmetic** $-0.051$M (CUB), $-5.79$M (SOP), $U$ = 1.05 MB.
7. **All frontier $z$/$p$ values** except the 35‑seed figure.
8. **The reduction at $G{=}1,K{=}C$** to a fixed regular‑simplex classifier, where $C-1\le d$.
9. **The honest disclosures** — §2.2's "Honest limit," the explicit SOP non‑crossing forecast, the refusal to claim CUB SOTA, §7's source ambiguities, and "I do not claim it adds information." These are accurate, and they are what decides the case.

## Uncertainty

I could not read the CVF PDFs for MDSHC and Deep Factorized Metric Learning (HTTP 403, same failure the proposal reports); my MDSHC characterization rests on the abstract and indexed summaries — the minimum‑distance‑constraint + Gilbert–Varshamov mechanism is confirmed from two surfaces, but I did not read the method section. The Codebook‑Centric/CRH fetch returned a truncated abstract; "preset codebook + dynamic feature‑coupled reassignment" is confirmed from the arXiv and AAAI listings, but I did not read its full algorithm, and whether its assignment is *balanced* is unverified. Dataset class counts (CUB 100/100, Cars 98/98, SOP 11,318/11,316, In‑Shop 3,997 train) are the standard DML splits and agree with the proposal's own figures; I did not check them against the repository. The nearest‑lattice‑cosine estimate ≈0.5 is a concentration estimate, not an exact covering radius. The memorization argument is an existence claim about representable global minimizers, not a claim about what SGD selects — but that is sufficient here, because §2.1's claim is precisely about the objective's *ranking* of solutions. I took the premise's PFML/PA+DADA anchors as given and did not adjudicate PFML's venue.

Any substantive repair — measuring the causal claim before proposing a cure, replacing C2/C4/C9 with feasible controls, smoothing the PMI term, re‑checking constraints after hysteresis, and running the In‑Shop‑first screen with a same‑seed PA control — is a new proposal, not a revision of this one.
