# In-Shop unseen-gallery training gate, 26 September 2026

The previous 128-D SigLIP2 bank candidate scored 95.4823% packed Recall@1 on official In-Shop query/gallery, below the dated UNICOM 96.7% reference. Its original TRAIN holdout overpredicted performance because the full TRAIN gallery contained fit-class distractors seen during training. [Evidence and diagnostic](inshop_siglip2_official_result_2026-09-25.md) establish the need for a gallery whose identities are all unseen during training. Official query/gallery labels and scores cannot select the new recipe.

The [frozen TRAIN-only preflight](evidence/compact_metric/sop-siglip2-substrate-v1/inshop-unseen-gallery-preflight-v1.json) divides 3,997 TRAIN products by a fixed identity hash, reserving every singleton for fit. It yields **13,283 fit images / 2,004 fit products** and **12,599 held images / 1,993 held products**. The held gallery is only 13 images smaller than the official 12,612-image gallery. All held images are symmetric queries with self excluded. This matches gallery size and identity novelty, though the official protocol is asymmetric and has more query/gallery products; only paired arm differences can select a method. Partition SHA-256 `cfada103c44df866db5e2ee9ecc2301ca691a4d0cdb3c875fe4051b62570894c`; preflight receipt SHA-256 `d5e22c6a331acbdf3b143b9836597593c2317f9488b99b72f61a4c54baf18eb8`.

The source-bound new trainer keeps the SigLIP2 Large/256 backbone, PCA-initialized 128-D head, 130-byte packed scorer, ArcFace coefficient and margin, detached member-bank SmoothAP coefficient 8, augmentation, AdamW rates/decay, BF16 precision, batch64 coverage-first identity schedule, singleton rank skip, and seed179023. The first 1,000 batches are identical in all arms. Three serial arms answer distinct questions:

| Arm | Change from control | TRAIN-only decisive readout |
| --- | --- | --- |
| control | 1,000 updates | baseline packed Recall@1 and mAP@R on the 12,599-image unseen gallery |
| freeze | 1,000 updates; freeze vision blocks 0–11 | same data and budget; tests whether fixing lower-block weights helps unseen identities and saves training time |
| budget | 3,000 updates; same trainable parameters | first 1,000 batches match control; tests whether undertraining explains the quality ceiling |

The prior fit saturation and block drift screens did not kill either arm: fit leave-one-out Recall@1 was 99.3613% (<99.5% threshold), and lower-block weight drift was 117% of upper (>25% threshold). The new gate uses product bootstrap on the paired per-query packed Recall@1 deltas. An arm advances only if its conditional 95% lower bound exceeds zero, mAP@R is no more than 0.005 below control, and training remains finite with complete source, schedule, checkpoint, memory and wall receipts. The one-seed gate is exploratory; a winner must replicate on independent seeds before algorithmic claims. If neither arm passes, no official read is repeated; diagnose final trained width/resolution or the local reproduction gap. If an arm passes, fix it using TRAIN only, then retrain on all official TRAIN products before the next clearly labelled exploratory official read. Native and full pipeline latency remain separate gates.

The **predeclared replication gate failed** because seed 179024's individual R@1 lower bound is below zero. Mean training wall fell from **1,145.4 to 983.7 s per 64,000 sampled images** (14.1% less); mean peak allocated CUDA memory fell from **22.554 to 18.124 GB** (19.6% less). Both new seeds clipped gradients on all 1,000 updates in both arms. In the original seed 179023 receipts, clipping was **not recorded**; the frozen readout's `clipped_steps: 0` for that seed must not be interpreted as zero clipping ([erratum](evidence/compact_metric/sop-siglip2-substrate-v1/inshop-unseen-gallery-seed-replication-erratum-v1.json)). The result supports a faster exploratory recipe with uncertain Recall@1 repeatability and an unresolved clipping mechanism; it does not establish a new learning-method or SOTA claim. The next TRAIN-only diagnostic compares the trained 1,024-D source with its 128-D float and packed head on the same unseen gallery, to locate the remaining quality ceiling before another training arm.

## Predeclared trained-width diagnostic

Use the exact seed-179023 freeze checkpoint and the same 12,599 official-TRAIN held images as both queries and self-excluded gallery. One encoder export captures its trained normalized 1,024-D pooler and 128-D normalized head on the same forward pass. Score float cosine top-1 for both and the existing 128-D packed scorer; require exact packed per-query Recall@1 and mAP@R parity with the frozen freeze receipt before interpreting gaps. Use the same fixed product bootstrap as the earlier gate. If source minus float head is at least **+0.5 percentage points** with positive conditional 95% lower bound, treat head geometry/width as the next production bottleneck. If the source gain is at most **+0.1 points**, including a negative gain, treat encoder/training as the next bottleneck. Otherwise the result is unresolved. The float-head minus packed comparison separately measures quantization cost. This is a one-checkpoint TRAIN-only diagnostic, not independent-seed confirmation or an official quality claim; no official read follows solely from it.

**Parity investigation:** two fresh exports failed exact receipt parity. The second differed on **4/12,599 packed top-1 hits** (fresh R@1 98.57925% versus receipt 98.59513%; fresh mAP@R 0.84065988 versus 0.84049463). Matching the trainer's disabled CUDA TF32 matmul left the same four discrepancies. A bounded rule had then accepted at most 10 top-1 mismatches and 0.0005 mAP@R drift. Independent review identified the actual checkpoint-loading defect: the diagnostic copied the trainer's FP32 checkpoint into an FP16 destination model *before* converting that model to FP32. The current diagnostic converts the destination first, asserts exact loaded tensor equality, and restores exact packed receipt parity as the gate. Earlier bounded receipts are archived as invalid diagnostic evidence.

The first bounded [receipt](evidence/compact_metric/sop-siglip2-substrate-v1/inshop-trained-width-179023-v1.json) has SHA-256 `115d0c17888c919cd5f75d9890cd4c0fa6a08273bfcca9a115861e700d652edc`; its [DGX Spark terminal journal](evidence/compact_metric/sop-siglip2-substrate-v1/inshop-trained-width-179023-v1.journal.log) has SHA-256 `b614d9b541770622654da9cb6f5dcae363933691b329bbd4fc65a738a24376e7`. It reported source/head/packed R@1 98.2697/98.5872/98.5793% on the 12,599-image held gallery, with source minus head −0.3175 points. These figures are **invalid for a trained-width decision** because the FP32 checkpoint was rounded during loading. In addition, even a faithful raw-pooler versus trained-head comparison cannot rule out a better *wider trained head*. It only compares the two representations actually exported.

The TF32-aligned but still rounded [receipt](evidence/compact_metric/sop-siglip2-substrate-v1/inshop-trained-width-179023-v3.json) has SHA-256 `e219de08b59dbab22cc07d3d0ca2cfd9f0c9c4f08a552939c8e46b364ec7ebc4`; its [journal](evidence/compact_metric/sop-siglip2-substrate-v1/inshop-trained-width-179023-v3.journal.log) has SHA-256 `c0b2e61ab3f0888ba640eb01b555d03c788830645c01299e72aa5536232a5004`. It reproduces the same numbers and four parity differences, confirming TF32 was not their cause.

**Provenance correction:** the `helper_sha256` fields for `export_all` and `score_packed_full_gallery` in the early receipts accidentally hash PyTorch's `inference_mode` decorator wrapper file, because `__code__.co_filename` points there. The actual imported helper module on DGX has SHA-256 `e2f7f8d16e2850a51aa85a3d3f4e04a80ee2a48681dcac55306fd792c8f19ba6`, matching the original training receipt. A source-bound replay explicitly validates and records that module hash.

The definitive FP32-faithful [TRAIN-only receipt](evidence/compact_metric/sop-siglip2-substrate-v1/inshop-trained-width-179023-v5.json) has SHA-256 `9f98ddf5bcced51b20e6f31aa752f61642d4bc7c4352872b15af8566502e65b1`; its [DGX Spark terminal journal](evidence/compact_metric/sop-siglip2-substrate-v1/inshop-trained-width-179023-v5.journal.log) has SHA-256 `12a457d8c6c2ea757f4876eb6405feedb412f363f060c58e0b43aaebef77bfd2`. The loader asserted every checkpoint tensor was copied exactly, the imported export/scorer helper module matches the original training SHA-256, and the packed replay matches the original receipt on **every per-query top-1 outcome and mAP@R exactly**. On the 12,599-image unseen-product official-TRAIN gallery, the trained raw 1,024-D float pooler scored **98.2697% R@1**, the trained 128-D float head **98.5872%**, and its 130-byte packed representation **98.5951%**. Raw source minus float head was **−0.3175 pp**, conditional product-bootstrap 95% interval **[−0.4976, −0.1445] pp**. Float head minus packed was **−0.00794 pp**, interval **[−0.0559, +0.0394] pp**. Thus packing has no demonstrated quality cost here, and this particular raw source offers no R@1 advantage over its trained head. A wider *trained* head was not tested. This is a single-checkpoint TRAIN-only diagnostic, not an official or SOTA result.

**Training decision correction:** both new control seeds had median pre-clip norms 68.8–71.2 against threshold 1.0, but their minima were 16.09–19.37. A threshold of 10 would still clip every recorded step. With AdamW, nearly constant gradient rescaling largely cancels in the first/second-moment ratio, so this arm poorly tests the hypothesis that parameter updates are too small. Do not run the clip-10 arm. After faithful checkpoint replay, a single preregistered vision learning-rate multiplier is a more direct production-oriented treatment; keep the 128-D packed scorer, fixed TRAIN-only split, schedules, and paired quality/cost gates. A fresh independent seed is required before claiming a repeatable method gain.

## Frozen vision learning-rate screen

The source-bound trainer SHA-256 is `d616513d3850b0268fa80ee28f2c16d4d852c0d25b889376650df72c5cb01290`. It adds only a bounded `--vision-lr` choice and records it in checkpoints and receipts. The paired control uses the prior vision rate **1e-5**; treatment uses **3e-5**, chosen once before treatment quality is read. Head/classifier rates remain 1e-4, AdamW decay 0.05, gradient clip 1, ArcFace + detached member-bank loss, BF16, 128-D head, 130-byte scorer, pretrained model, product-disjoint split, and seed-specific 1,000-batch schedules remain fixed. This tests the effect of a larger vision optimizer step on unseen-product retrieval, not a novel algorithm or a general learning-rate optimum.

First run the serial 17-update control/treatment smoke for seed 179024. It must show identical first-ten input and schedule hashes, source/model/PCA initialization, finite losses, and an exact default-rate control checkpoint tensor replay against the old seed-179024 control smoke; the treatment must change at least one vision encoder tensor relative to this control. No retrieval quality is read at 17 updates. If the smoke fails, diagnose before a full run. Then run a new same-source, same-session 1,000-update control followed by treatment on seed 179024; compare their packed per-query R@1 with the fixed product bootstrap and mAP@R. Stop without seed 179025 if treatment is nonfinite, its R@1 lower 95% bound is at most zero, its mAP@R drops more than 0.005, or its training wall or peak allocated CUDA memory exceed control by more than 10%. If seed 179024 passes, repeat the same serial pair for seed 179025; both seeds must pass individually to advance. These seeds were inspected when choosing this treatment, so even two passes remain exploratory; a fresh, preregistered seed and split-stable paired control are required for confirmation. No official query/gallery read follows a failed screen. Report exact raw receipts, source/input hashes, training wall/images per second/VRAM, packed quality and latency separately. The existing official In-Shop 96.7% published UNICOM R@1 remains a reference gate, not a claim.
